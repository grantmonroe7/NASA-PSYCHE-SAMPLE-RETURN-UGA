# Low Gravity, Launch/Return Loads, and Environments

## Surface Gravity
- Surface gravity ~0.14 m s⁻² (very roughly 0.015 g).
- Weight-on-bit must be generated by the spacecraft / lander / anchoring system or by the drill’s own reaction management (e.g., the cable-pulley system already designed).
- Anchoring or multi-point contact is essential; pure spacecraft mass is insufficient for high WOB.

## Launch and Return Loads
- Random vibration and quasi-static loads from launch vehicle and Earth-return entry/landing (or sample-container handling).
- Components must be designed and tested to NASA GEVS or equivalent random-vibration spectra, plus shock.
- Modal analysis required to keep natural frequencies away from high-energy launch bands.
- Thermal cycling (deep-space cruise + surface diurnal) and electromagnetic environment (spacecraft EMI + possible natural fields) must be considered for motors, sensors, and harness.

## Countermeasures Already Aligned with Existing Drives
- Low-g force generation via motorised cable + pulley advantage (penetration drive).
- Cold-start jogging for thermal conditioning of actuators.
- Robust sequencing that can reduce force or switch mode if anomalous torque is detected.
- Structural analysis (this drive) supplies the load cases and environmental envelopes for FEA / vibration testing of the physical hardware.
