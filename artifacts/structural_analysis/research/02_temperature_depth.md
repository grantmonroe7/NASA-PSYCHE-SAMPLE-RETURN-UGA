# Surface and Subsurface Temperature

## Observational Constraints
- Diurnal temperature variations ~10 K over most of the surface (ALMA).
- Thermal inertia estimates span a wide range:
  - Low end: 5–25 J m⁻² K⁻¹ s⁻¹/² (fine silicate regolith interpretation)
  - Intermediate: ~100–150
  - Higher: ~280 ± 100 (mm-wave)
- Heterogeneous: lowlands of large depressions show distinctly lower thermal inertia.

## Skin Depths & Depth Profile
Thermal skin depth δ ≈ √(κ P / π) where P is rotation period (~4.2 h) or orbital period.
- Diurnal skin depth for low-TI fine regolith: centimetres or less.
- Annual / seasonal skin depth: metres-scale, but amplitude strongly damped.

**Practical implication for drilling**:
- The top few cm experience large diurnal swings.
- Below ~10–50 cm (depending on local TI) temperature is closer to the diurnal mean and more stable.
- Polar / high-latitude regions and permanently shadowed pockets (if any) can be substantially colder.

## Heating During Drilling
Metal-rich targets conduct heat well; silicate regolith is a poor conductor. Frictional and cutting heat will raise local temperatures. For icy or hydrated components (minor OH detections exist) this could cause sublimation or alteration. For pure metal/silicate the main concerns are tool wear, lubricant behaviour, and motor thermal limits rather than sample melting.

**Sources**: de Kleer et al., Cambioni et al., Landsman et al., Matter et al., Sori et al. (thermal cracking / porosity).
