# Sampling Condition, Heating, Extremes, and Mass Return

## Expected Sample Condition
- **Loose / porous regolith regions**: cuttings, powdered or weakly aggregated metal–silicate mixture; may be recovered as bulk regolith rather than intact core.
- **More competent metal-rich or mixed units**: possible solid or fractured core segments if the drill can maintain integrity; metal is ductile and may smear or work-harden.
- Wireline or multi-string systems should be prepared for both powdered return and occasional coherent pieces.

## Heating Considerations
- Frictional heating at the bit is the dominant local heat source.
- Metal conducts heat away efficiently; fine silicate regolith does not.
- Minor hydrated phases (if present) are vulnerable to thermal alteration / dehydration.
- Recommendation: limit continuous high-power intervals; use intermittent or percussive modes; monitor bit temperature if possible; prefer cold-start / low-duty strategies already implemented in the penetration drive.

## Preparing for Extremes
| Extreme | Design response |
|---------|-----------------|
| Loose, low-cohesion soil | Low weight-on-bit, high-speed or percussive modes; prevent borehole collapse with casing or careful extraction sequencing |
| Hard metal / competent bedrock | High-torque low-speed (LVHT) mode; robust bit materials; torque and current limiting; possible percussion to initiate fracture |
| Mixed / unknown | Adaptive sequencing (already in master sequence); real-time torque / penetration-rate feedback |

## Maximizing Return Mass while Preserving Integrity
- Prefer multiple short cores or segmented samples over one long core that risks jamming or contamination.
- Wireline retrieval minimises time the sample spends in a hot or contaminated borehole.
- Use core catchers and stabilisers suited to both granular and coherent metal-rich material.
- Mass budget: metal-rich samples are dense; volume is the limiting factor more than mass for a given container.
