# Density and Material Composition

## Bulk Asteroid
- Preferred bulk density range: **3.4–4.2 g cm⁻³** (recent high-precision astrometry ~4.17 ± 0.15 g cm⁻³).
- Pure Fe-Ni metal (~7.8–7.9 g cm⁻³) would require **~40–55 % porosity** to match bulk density.
- Mixed models preferred: **~30–60 vol% metal**, remainder low-Fe silicate + porosity ≤ ~20–40 %.

## Upper Meter (Radar)
- Radar albedo 0.34 ± 0.08 → regolith bulk density roughly 2.6–4.8 g cm⁻³.
- Consistent with metal-bearing, porous regolith or lower-porosity metal–silicate mixtures.

## Surface Mineralogy
- Metal (Fe-Ni).
- Low-Fe orthopyroxene (0.9 µm band, 1–3 % depth, spatially variable).
- Possible hydrated / carbonaceous exogenic component (3 µm feature).
- Lab mixture best-fit example: ~82.5 % metal + 7 % low-Fe pyroxene + 10.5 % carbonaceous by weight (Cantillo et al.), implying ~35 % porosity if representative of bulk.

## Implications for Drilling
- Expect both loose, porous, metal-rich regolith **and** more competent metal-rich or silicate-bearing units.
- Strength and abrasiveness will vary strongly with local metal fraction and porosity.
- Rubble-pile or macroporous models remain viable; hydrocode cratering studies favour some macroscopic void space.

**Sources**: Elkins-Tanton et al. (2020), Farnocchia et al., Shepard et al., Cantillo et al., hydrocode porosity studies.
